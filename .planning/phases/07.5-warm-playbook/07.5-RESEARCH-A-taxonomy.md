**Researched:** 2026-08-18

## 0. Design frame — why a driver taxonomy, not just outcomes

The move-registry (Phase 6.12) already stores WHAT happened (signed T-1→T+2 move) and WHEN (knowable-as-of). The playbook's job is to store **WHY** in a form a model can reason over by analogy. That means every past catalyst becomes a record of:

1. **Outcome label** (enum) + **realized signed move** + **confidence** + **citation** — already in scope.
2. **Causal drivers** — the structured, pre-decision-observable factors that *explain/predict* the outcome. This document defines them.
3. **What-to-watch tells** — the leading indicators a forward analyst monitors as the catalyst approaches (the no-look-ahead-safe subset the application layer is allowed to use).

The taxonomy below is organized by the three catalyst types this fund trades: **PDUFA/FDA decision**, **trial readout**, and **AdCom**. For each driver: *what it is · why it matters · how it's observable pre-decision · directional weight · source*.

A structural note that shapes everything: **outcomes are only weakly predictable and the base rates are low.** Overall Phase-I-to-approval likelihood is ~7.9% (BIO 2011–2020) and ~6.7% in the most recent 2014–2023 window; even a *statistically significant* Phase 2 replicates in Phase 3 only ~43% of the time at p=0.05 ([Wong/Siah/Lo](https://academic.oup.com/biostatistics/article/20/2/273/4817524); [BIO 2011–2020](https://go.bio.org/rs/490-EHZ-999/images/ClinicalDevelopmentSuccessRates2011_2020.pdf); [reproducibility analysis](https://www.redjournal.org/article/S0360-3016(25)05892-4/fulltext)). The playbook is therefore a **base-rate + adjustment** engine, not an oracle: start from the class base rate, then move it with the drivers below. This is exactly the shape the KB's `fitted_params:<type>` seam wants.

## 1. PDUFA / FDA DECISION drivers (Approve vs CRL vs delay)

**Outcome enum:** `approve` · `approve_with_restrictions` (REMS/narrow label/boxed warning) · `crl` · `delay` (extension of PDUFA goal date, e.g. major amendment → +3 months) · `withdrawn` (sponsor pulls before decision).

**Class base rate to anchor from:** Once a program reaches NDA/BLA filing, ~85–92% eventually reach the market ([phase-transition synthesis](https://ibinterviewquestions.com/guides/healthcare-investment-banking/probability-of-success-by-phase)). So the *prior* on a first-cycle-clean approval is high but the *first-cycle CRL rate is material* — CMC/manufacturing alone drives ~29% of CRL deficiencies (see driver D6). The playbook's job is to separate the ~10–15% that CRL/delay from the majority that approve.

| # | Driver | What it is / why it matters | Pre-decision observable? | Directional weight |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| D1 | **AdCom vote** (if held) | Panel recommendation is the single strongest public tell. FDA agrees with the committee ~86–90% of the time (2010–2015: 90%; 2017–2022: 86%). A favorable vote → approval is near-certain; an unfavorable vote still approves ~36% of the time, so a "no" is a strong-but-not-fatal negative. | Yes — vote is public same-day, days-to-months before PDUFA. | **Very high.** Favorable ≈ +; Unfavorable ≈ strong − (but not absolute). [Zhang 2010–2015](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1544319118302395); [JAMA 2010–2021](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10329213/) |
| D2 | **Strength & consistency of pivotal data** | Two adequate & well-controlled trials, or one very strong trial + confirmatory evidence; internal consistency across subgroups/secondary endpoints; effect size vs. bar. Weak/inconsistent efficacy → clinical-opinion CRL. | Yes — topline PRs, published pivotal results, prior readouts in the KB. | **High.** Strong/consistent ≈ +; single marginal trial ≈ −. Clinical-opinion disagreements are 43% of FDA↔AdCom splits. [JAMA](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10329213/) |
| D3 | **Endpoint acceptability** | Did pivotal use an FDA-accepted endpoint (hard clinical outcome, or an established/validated surrogate) vs. a novel surrogate FDA hasn't blessed? | D4 | **Safety signal / therapeutic-window** | Serious AEs, deaths, hepatotoxicity, imbalance vs. control, or a benefit-risk that's tight for the indication severity. Safety is ~13% of CRL deficiencies but disproportionately fatal for benign-indication drugs. | Yes — trial AE tables, prior CRL text, FDA briefing docs (released ~2 days pre-AdCom). | **High (asymmetric).** A clean safety profile is table-stakes; a live safety signal is a strong −. [CRL analysis](https://www.parexel.com/insights/blog/understanding-fda-complete-response-letters-why-cmc-readiness-must-become-a-strategic-priority) |
| D5 | **Prior CRL history (this asset)** | A resubmission after a CRL whose cited deficiencies were addressable (CMC, label, one extra trial) often approves on the next cycle; a CRL over fundamental efficacy rarely recovers. The *reason* in the prior CRL is the signal, not the CRL itself. | Yes — prior CRL 8-K/PR text (often disclosed), resubmission class (Class 1 = +2mo, Class 2 = +6mo). | **High, sign depends on CRL reason.** Addressable-CRL resubmission ≈ +; efficacy-CRL ≈ −. [c&en](https://cen.acs.org/articles/95/i20/complete-response-letter.html) |
| D6 | **CMC / manufacturing readiness** | Facility inspection status, process validation, third-party API sites, prior 483s/warning letters. **CMC is the single most common CRL cause (~29% of deficiencies) — larger than clinical safety + efficacy combined.** Often invisible to the science thesis. | Partly — inspection history (FDA databases), prior 483s, CDMO reliance, sponsor's manufacturing track record. Hard to fully observe. | **High, under-priced.** Weak CMC ≈ strong −; the "surprise CRL on a good drug" is usually here. [Parexel](https://www.parexel.com/insights/blog/understanding-fda-complete-response-letters-why-cmc-readiness-must-become-a-strategic-priority); [Pharm Tech](https://www.pharmtech.com/view/cmc-and-analytical-gaps-in-crls) |
| D7 | **Review division track record** | Divisions differ in stringency and precedent (e.g. oncology/CBER-gene-therapy vs. neurology/psychiatry). A division with a history of approving-around-a-surrogate in the class raises odds; a division that has repeatedly demanded confirmatory outcomes lowers them. | Yes — division's recent approvals/CRLs in the same class (from the KB itself). | **Medium.** Favorable-division precedent ≈ +. Division inference from FDA action history. |
| D8 | **Precedent approvals in class / MoA validation** | Is this a first-in-class MoA (regulatory uncertainty ↑, CRL risk ↑) or a follow-on to an already-approved mechanism (de-risked)? Genome-targeted / mechanistically-validated MoAs validate benefit and reach regular approval more often. | Yes — class approval history (KB), MoA precedent. | **Medium-high.** Validated MoA / in-class precedent ≈ +; first-in-class ≈ − (uncertainty). [BTD/genome-targeted](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11350476/) |
| D9 | **Expedited designations** (Fast Track / Breakthrough / Priority Review / Accelerated Approval) | Signals FDA already sees substantial-improvement preliminary evidence (BTD) and unmet need. BTD is associated with lower withdrawal rates and more regular-approval conversion; Priority Review = 6-month clock. Designations are a *revealed-preference* positive from the agency itself. | Yes — announced via 8-K/PR, on FDA lists. | **Medium.** BTD/Priority ≈ +; note Accelerated Approval carries post-marketing-withdrawal tail risk. [FDA expedited programs](https://www.fda.gov/patients/learn-about-drug-and-device-approvals/fast-track-breakthrough-therapy-accelerated-approval-priority-review) |
| D10 | **Label / commercial negotiation state** | Late label disputes (indication breadth, boxed warning, REMS) can convert an approval into a delay or a restricted approval that the market treats as a partial miss. | Partly — management commentary, FDA communications tone, briefing-doc labeling section. | **Medium (magnitude, not direction).** Drives approve vs. approve-with-restrictions and the *size* of the move. [CRL/labeling](https://casrai.org/guides/fda-complete-response-letter-crl) |

**Storable fields (PDUFA record):** `adcom_held`(bool), `adcom_vote`(for-against), `pivotal_n_trials`, `pivotal_effect_consistency`(enum), `endpoint_type`(hard/validated-surrogate/novel-surrogate), `safety_flag`(enum + description), `prior_crl`(bool + reason enum), `resubmission_class`(1/2), `cmc_risk`(enum, inspection/483 history), `review_division`, `class_precedent`(count approved in class), `moa_validated`(bool), `designations`(set), `label_dispute`(bool). Plus outcome/move/confidence/citation.

## 2. TRIAL READOUT drivers (Hit / Miss / Mixed)

**Outcome enum:** `hit` (primary met, clean) · `miss` (primary failed) · `mixed` (primary met but secondaries/safety muddy, or subgroup-only) · `hit_underwhelming` (met stat-sig but effect below the commercial/market bar).

**Class base rates to anchor from (phase-conditioned — this is the prior the playbook adjusts):**

| Transition | Success rate | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Phase 1 → 2 | ~52–65% (recent ~47%) | [phase synthesis](https://ibinterviewquestions.com/guides/healthcare-investment-banking/probability-of-success-by-phase); [Norstella 2014–2023](https://www.norstella.com/insight/why-are-clinical-development-success-rates-falling/) |
| **Phase 2 → 3** | **~28–35% (the attrition cliff)** | [BIO](https://go.bio.org/rs/490-EHZ-999/images/ClinicalDevelopmentSuccessRates2011_2020.pdf); [immunology 30.8%](https://www.jacionline.org/article/S0091-6749(17)30753-4/fulltext) |
| Phase 3 → NDA/BLA | ~55–60% | Wong/Siah/Lo; phase synthesis |
| NDA/BLA → approval | ~85–92% | phase synthesis |
| **Phase 1 → approval (aggregate)** | **~7.9% (2011–20); ~6.7% (2014–23)** | [BIO](https://go.bio.org/rs/490-EHZ-999/images/ClinicalDevelopmentSuccessRates2011_2020.pdf); [Norstella](https://www.norstella.com/insight/why-are-clinical-development-success-rates-falling/) |
| By modality | Biologics 9.1% · NMEs (small molecule) 5.7% · Vaccines 9.7% | [BIO](https://go.bio.org/rs/490-EHZ-999/images/ClinicalDevelopmentSuccessRates2011_2020.pdf) |
| Oncology Ph1→approval | ~3.3% (range 0–10.1% by disease group); **+13.3% avg if biomarker-selected** | [Wong/Siah/Lo oncology](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=3355022) |

| # | Driver | What it is / why it matters | Pre-readout observable? | Directional weight |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| R1 | **Prior-phase effect size & p-value** | The setup evidence. But a critical, counter-intuitive weight: **statistical significance in Ph2 is a weak predictor of Ph3.** Effects at p=0.05 replicate ~43% of the time; p=0.01 ~60%. A *large, robust* Ph2 effect matters more than a *barely-significant* one. | Yes — prior readout PR/publication in KB. | **Medium (not high).** Large clean effect ≈ +; marginal p≈0.05 ≈ near-coin-flip. [reproducibility](https://www.redjournal.org/article/S0360-3016(25)05892-4/fulltext); [ADR "why pivotals fail"](https://www.adrclinresearch.com/index_htm_files/Why%20Pivotal%20Clinical%20Trials%20Fail%20-%20Part%201_v12L_a.pdf) |
| R2 | **Endpoint design: hard outcome vs. surrogate** | Hard clinical endpoints (OS, MACE, event-free survival) are harder to hit but harder to argue with; surrogate endpoints (ORR, PFS, biomarker) hit more often but carry FDA/market discount. Endpoint *hardness* trades off hit-probability against outcome value. | Yes — CT.gov primary/secondary endpoint definitions. | **High (shapes both P(hit) and magnitude).** |
| R3 | **Powering / sample size / design quality** | Underpowered or design-flawed pivotals fail even on real drugs; a big reason Ph3 fails despite Ph2 promise is design/implementation, not biology. Adequate powering off a *realistic* (not Ph2-inflated) effect assumption is protective. | Yes — CT.gov enrollment, statistical analysis plan, powering assumptions vs. Ph2 effect. | **Medium-high.** Overpowered-on-inflated-Ph2 ≈ −; conservatively powered ≈ +. [ADR](https://www.adrclinresearch.com/index_htm_files/Why%20Pivotal%20Clinical%20Trials%20Fail%20-%20Part%201_v12L_a.pdf) |
| R4 | **Mechanism validation / class precedent** | Has the MoA already produced a positive readout in this or an adjacent indication? Validated mechanisms de-risk; first-in-mechanism readouts carry the full biology risk. Overlaps hedge-peer selection (a validated-MoA peer is the natural hedge). | Yes — class readout history (KB), competitor reads. | **Medium-high.** Validated ≈ +; novel ≈ −. |
| R5 | **Patient population / enrichment** | Biomarker-selected or enriched populations raise PoS materially (**+13.3% avg in oncology**). Broad all-comer populations dilute effect and miss more. | Yes — CT.gov eligibility, enrichment strategy. | **High in oncology / targeted; medium elsewhere.** [Wong/Siah/Lo](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=3355022) |
| R6 | **Prior interim looks / DSMB actions** | An interim efficacy pass, a "continue" from an independent DSMB, or a sample-size re-estimation are weak-positive tells; a stopped-for-futility signal is decisive. Absence of a futility stop on an adaptive design is mildly reassuring. | Partly — DSMB actions sometimes disclosed via 8-K/PR. | **Medium (asymmetric).** Futility stop ≈ strong −; passed interim ≈ mild +. |
| R7 | **Therapeutic-area base rate** | The prior itself varies ~3× by area (oncology low, some rare/metabolic higher). The playbook must condition the base rate on TA before applying drivers. | Yes — indication → TA mapping. | **High as the prior; not an adjustment.** [BIO](https://go.bio.org/rs/490-EHZ-999/images/ClinicalDevelopmentSuccessRates2011_2020.pdf) |
| R8 | **Sponsor / program track record** | Sponsors with prior successful readouts in the class, and larger/experienced sponsors, transition at higher rates. Serial-failure programs and pivot-heavy protocols are negative. | Yes — sponsor history (KB). | **Low-medium.** |

**Storable fields (readout record):** `phase`, `transition`(e.g. Ph2→Ph3), `ta_base_rate`, `modality`(biologic/NME/vaccine), `prior_effect_size`, `prior_pvalue`, `endpoint_type`(hard/surrogate), `powering`(adequate/inflated), `moa_validated`(bool), `population`(all-comer/enriched/biomarker), `interim_look`(enum), `dsmb_action`, `sponsor_track`(enum). Plus outcome/move/confidence/citation.

## 3. ADCOM drivers (favorable vs. unfavorable vote)

AdCom is both a *catalyst in itself* (the vote moves the stock) and the strongest predictor of the downstream PDUFA (D1). Committees vote favorably ~67% of the time and against ~33% ([JAMA](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10329213/)).

**Outcome enum:** `favorable` · `unfavorable` · `split` (close vote — market treats as soft-negative).

| # | Driver | What it is / why it matters | Observable? | Weight |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A1 | **FDA briefing-document tone** | FDA's own briefing docs (released ~2 business days pre-AdCom) reveal the agency's posture. A skeptical FDA review memo is the strongest pre-vote tell — the panel usually tracks the agency's framing. | Yes — public ~48h before. | **Very high.** Skeptical FDA memo ≈ strong −. |
| A2 | **Benefit-risk clarity for indication severity** | Panels weigh effect size against unmet need and safety. A modest effect is forgiven in a fatal orphan indication, punished in a benign chronic one. | Yes — trial data + indication context. | **High.** |
| A3 | **Endpoint / surrogate acceptability** | Same as D3 — panels vote down contested surrogates and post-hoc/subgroup-driven wins. Clean prespecified primary ≈ +. | Yes. | **High.** |
| A4 | **Safety signal salience** | A visible safety signal (deaths, organ tox) dominates panel discussion and drives no-votes even with efficacy. | Yes — briefing docs, trial AE tables. | **High (asymmetric −).** |
| A5 | **Advocacy / unmet-need pressure** | Patient-advocacy testimony and total absence of alternatives push panels toward yes (documented in AA/neurology cases). | Partly — public docket, indication landscape. | **Medium (+).** |
| A6 | **Panel composition** | Statistician-heavy or historically-stringent panels vote harder on data quality; conflict-of-interest waivers matter little empirically. | Partly — roster sometimes pre-published. | **Low-medium.** [RAPS COI](https://www.raps.org/resource/conflicts-of-interest-on-fda-s-advisory-committees.html) |

**Storable fields (AdCom record):** `briefing_doc_tone`(enum), `benefit_risk`(enum vs. indication severity), `endpoint_contested`(bool), `safety_salient`(bool), `advocacy_pressure`(enum), `vote_split`(n_for–n_against). The **downstream link** is the key structural edge: an AdCom record `RESULTED_IN` the vote, and the vote feeds D1 of the same asset's PDUFA record.

## 5. How event-driven / biotech desks structure catalyst knowledge (+ the LLM/RAG pattern)

**How real desks catalogue it.** Event-driven biotech funds run a **catalyst-calendar + post-mortem** discipline: they take small positions across many upcoming catalysts (10–20 events/quarter) to diversify binary risk, and they maintain a structured record of each past catalyst's setup, outcome, and *why* — precisely the "separate evidence from expectations" framework (valuation, pre-event move, options pricing, analyst language, short interest, competitor comparison) that this taxonomy formalizes ([BiopharmaWatch hedge-fund positioning](https://www.biopharmawatch.com/blog/biotech-catalyst-trading-hedge-funds-insiders-fda-decisions); [Aurum event-driven primer](https://www.aurum.com/insight/thought-piece/event-driven-hedge-fund-strategies-explained/); [BioCatalysts playbook](https://biocatalysts.ai/learn/biotech-catalyst-trading-strategies)). The reusable unit is a **case**: setup → drivers → outcome → realized move → lesson. That is exactly the KB's `catalyst → prediction(setup) → RESULTED_IN → outcome` shape with a `playbook` generalization node.

**The modern LLM pattern — RAG "updated context".** The current best practice for giving an LLM decision-support memory is retrieval-augmented generation: keep a knowledge base of prior cases, embed each, and at decision time retrieve the most *analogous* prior cases and inject them as context, so the model reasons from evidence rather than parametric memory (mitigating hallucination + knowledge-cutoff). This is now applied specifically to investment briefs and fundamental analysis ([RAG-based investor-brief system, arXiv 2607.09121](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.09121v1); [FinDER financial RAG benchmark](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2504.15800); [financial-report RAG evaluation, MDPI](https://www.mdpi.com/2076-3417/14/20/9318)). Retrieval-by-analogy + reranking is the documented pattern for "precise, contextually relevant inputs for LLMs in financial tasks."

**What each KB item should capture (synthesized to our schema):**

- **Identity/filter fields** (for structural pre-filter before vector search): catalyst_type, indication/TA, modality, MoA class, phase, review division, designations, market-cap bucket, date + `knowable_asof`.
- **Setup / drivers**: the type-specific driver fields from §1–§3 (the values as they *were pre-decision*).
- **Watch-list**: the §4 tells with their pre-event readings.
- **Outcome**: enum label + realized signed move (T-1→T+2) + confidence + **citation** (cite-or-drop, per `insert_claim`).
- **Generalization (`playbook` node)**: `scenario_type`, `scenario`, `strategy`, `confidence`, evidence — the reusable lesson (`insert_playbook`), plus per-cohort `fitted_params:<type>` (`seed_fitted_params`).
- **Embedding text** (what `analog_query` embeds): a natural-language digest of type + indication + MoA + driver values + setup, so vector similarity retrieves *mechanistically/regulatorily analogous* prior catalysts, then the structural filter constrains to type/indication-conditioned and `<= as_of` (no-look-ahead at application).

**How it's retrieved by analogy (maps to shipped `analog_query`):** embed the live catalyst's driver digest → cosine top-k over `kb_vec` → structural filter (same catalyst_type, matching/adjacent indication+MoA, `knowable_asof <= decision_date`) → attach each analog's outcome via `RESULTED_IN` → format the k analogs (drivers + outcome + realized move + watch-list) as the "updated context" block fed to the analyst desk, alongside the class base rate (§1–§2) and the football-field estimator. The base rate is the prior; the retrieved analogs and their drivers are the adjustment.

## 6. Consolidated storable schema (fields the playbook records must carry)

Common to every record (already supported by `kb.py`): `catalyst_id`, `company`, `catalyst_type`, `indication`, `ta`, `modality`, `moa_class`, `phase`, `review_division`, `designations[]`, `mktcap_bucket`, `event_date`, `knowable_asof`, `outcome_label`(enum), `realized_move`(signed, window), `confidence`(0–1), `sources[]`(cite-or-drop), `embedding_text`, plus a `watch_list[]` and a link to a generalizing `playbook` node + `fitted_params:<type>` cohort.

Type-specific driver blocks: **PDUFA** = D1–D10 fields (§1). **Readout** = R1–R8 fields (§2). **AdCom** = A1–A6 fields (§3).

**Directional-weight priors** (to seed `fitted_params`, then refit per cohort — do not hardcode as law): PDUFA — AdCom vote (very high), pivotal strength / endpoint acceptability / safety / CMC / prior-CRL-reason (high), division/class-precedent/designations (medium). Readout — TA base rate + endpoint hardness + biomarker enrichment (high), powering / MoA validation (medium-high), prior effect *magnitude* > prior p-value (medium; significance ≠ replication). AdCom — briefing-doc tone (very high), benefit-risk / endpoint / safety (high).
