# 07.5 RESEARCH D — The NARRATIVE / SEMANTIC layer (how the pros write "what happened and why")

**Scope:** Design Q1 (content/schema — the *narrative* half). Siblings A (driver taxonomy),
B (attribution), C (storage/retrieval) cover the structured-field half. This doc covers the layer
they don't: the natural-language fields that explain **what happened and WHY**, situated in industry
history, regulatory precedent, management track record, and competitive dynamics — the part a real
analyst writes in prose, and the part that makes a past catalyst *reusable as precedent* for the next
one.

**Where it lands in the existing scaffold:** these are string fields inside the `catalyst` node's and
`outcome` node's `attrs_json` (`kb.py:66`), alongside the structured drivers. The `catalyst` node
already carries "the retrieval text" (RESEARCH-C §0) — the narrative fields below *are* the richer
version of that retrieval text, plus a post-mortem block on the `outcome` node. No schema change: it's
all `attrs_json`. One narrative field (`thesis`/context string) is what gets embedded into `kb_vec`.

---

## 0. Why a narrative layer at all (the design gap)

Our playbook today is enums + numbers: `outcome=CRL`, `signed_move=-0.42`, `endpoint_met=false`.
That is necessary but not sufficient. Two catalysts can share every structured field yet be completely
different precedents — a CRL for a manufacturing/CMC deficiency at a third-party fill-finish site is a
*near-term, fixable, Class-1-resubmission* story; a CRL that says "your pivotal design no longer
supports approval" is an *existential, new-trial-required* story. Same enum, opposite implication for
the next trade. The distinction lives **only in prose**. The pros solved this long ago, and the
solution is remarkably consistent across value investing, macro, and behavioral-decision science:
capture the **causal story**, the **base-rate-vs-this-case reasoning**, the **variant view**, the
**disconfirmers**, and — after resolution — a **process-not-outcome post-mortem**.

The rest of this doc is what those five things look like in practice, cited, then a concrete field spec.

---

## 1. Hedge-fund / equity-research playbooks & memos — what narrative fields the pros capture

### 1a. The investment-thesis memo (the "before" narrative)

The canonical memo is not a data dump; it's an *argument* with a fixed skeleton. Across VC, PE and
hedge-fund practice the recurring narrative sections are: a 2–3-sentence **thesis in the executive
summary**, a **problem/situation**, the **variant view**, a **valuation/expectation gap**, a
**steelmanned bear case**, and an explicit **"what has to be true"**
([Daloopa hedge-fund memo](https://daloopa.com/blog/analyst-best-practices/hedge-fund-investment-memo-example);
[Sutton Capital template](https://suttoncapital.co/blog/investment-memo-template);
[Stanford/Addepar brief on memos & decision-making](https://longterminvesting.stanford.edu/sites/g/files/sbiybj23856/files/media/file/addepar-investment-memos-and-decision-making.pdf)).

Two compact formats worth stealing wholesale (both from the Addepar/hedge-fund practitioner writeups):

- **The decision brief** — one page answering three questions: *what do we believe*, *what would we
  need to be wrong about to pass*, and *what has to be true for this to be a fund-returner*.
- **The partner note** — an informal letter structured as *"here is what I saw, here is what I think,
  here is what I recommend."*

Both are pure narrative and both are tiny. That is the target register for our fields: a paragraph, not
a page.

### 1b. The Value Investors Club write-up (the gold-standard pattern library)

VIC is the closest public analog to what our playbook *is*: a searchable library of one-idea write-ups,
each disciplined to a fixed narrative shape. The committee's bar is explicitly a **variant-perception**
bar: *"if the street could have published it, it won't pass… you must pitch something the market has
gotten wrong/misinterpreted for XYZ reasons, plus a catalyst that will make the market realize its
mistake and re-rate."* And it demands a **steelmanned bear case**: *"a bear case that genuinely
threatens the thesis — steelman it, then explain why you own it anyway"*
([WSO: VIC critique thread](https://www.wallstreetoasis.com/forum/hedge-fund/how-do-you-get-into-vic-please-critique-my-write-up);
[ThematicEdge: Best of VIC](https://thematicedge.substack.com/p/the-best-of-the-legendary-value-investors);
[SpecialSituationInvestments VIC summaries](https://www.specialsituationinvestments.com/vic/)).
Prized values: *clarity, concision, no jargon.* Our narrative fields should read like a good VIC
write-up compressed to a few sentences each.

### 1c. Mauboussin: expectations & the base-rate/reference-class discipline

Mauboussin gives the *content* of the "why," in two moves:

1. **Expectations / variant perception.** *"Perhaps the single greatest error in the investment
   business is a failure to distinguish between the knowledge of a company's fundamentals and the
   expectations implied by the market price."* The thesis is not "the drug is good," it's *"the drug is
   better/worse than what the price implies, and here is the specific gap."*
   ([Expectations Investing Q&A, Motley Fool](https://www.fool.com/investing/2022/01/19/expectations-investing-qanda-mauboussin-rappaport/);
   [Financial Pen on variant perception](https://thefinancialpen.substack.com/p/variant-perception-the-mechanics)).
   For a biotech binary this maps directly to *implied move / priced-in probability of success vs our
   estimate* — a narrative sentence, backed by C's structured numbers.

2. **Outside view via a reference class.** Choose a class *"large enough to be statistically useful but
   narrow enough to be applicable,"* combine the inside view (this drug's specifics) with the base rate
   of that class, and *"track your decisions in a journal, reviewing outcomes against base rates."*
   ([The Base Rate Book summary, HedgeFundAlpha](https://hedgefundalpha.com/education/michael-mauboussin-the-base-rate-book/);
   [Compounding Quality/CompCap on base rates](https://compcap.substack.com/p/michael-mauboussin-base-rates)).
   This is the single most important reusability idea in the doc: **every narrative should name its
   reference class and state the base rate, so a future analyst can retrieve "cases like this" and
   inherit the prior.** (This is exactly what `analog_query` retrieves; the narrative makes it legible.)

### 1d. Bridgewater: the case-study / issue-log method

Bridgewater's contribution is the *institutional loop*, not the memo. The **Issue Log** records every
mistake so problems "get into the hands of problem-solvers to make **systematic** improvements," and
the **Pain Button** records the emotion, then walks the person through structured reflection and a
*plan to prevent the pain next time*, with analytics on frequency and cause
([Shortform: Bridgewater tools](https://www.shortform.com/blog/bridgewater-tools/);
[Shortform: Dalio on mistakes](https://www.shortform.com/blog/ray-dalio-mistakes/);
[principles.com](https://www.principles.com/)). The reusable idea: an outcome record is only valuable
if it names a **root cause** and a **generalizable rule** ("machine-level" fix), not just "we were
wrong." Our post-mortem block must produce a one-line *rule for next time*, which is what promotes a
catalyst-outcome into a `playbook` node.

## 2. The "WHY" narrative — what makes a causal explanation *reusable as precedent*

A number ("−42%") is not precedent; a *causal story situated in precedent* is. From the CRL literature,
a good "why" for a biotech catalyst names the **deficiency class** and situates it against **what
usually happens to that class next**. The FDA itself frames a CRL as *"not a final rejection — it
describes specific deficiencies and, when possible, recommends actions,"* and the deficiency class is
what governs the sequel: CMC/GMP-only CRLs are frequently curable via a **Class-1 resubmission (2-month
review)**, whereas efficacy/trial-design CRLs trigger a **Class-2 (6-month) or a whole new trial**
([Avalere: what is a CRL](https://advisory.avalerehealth.com/insights/what-is-a-complete-response-letter);
[RTTNews: CRL & biotech stocks](https://www.rttnews.com/amp/3662437/fda-complete-response-letter-crl-explained-what-it-means-for-biotech-stocks.aspx);
[PharmaDossier CRL guide](https://pharmadossier.com/blog/fda-complete-response-letter-guide)).

Two real 2025–26 cases show the *range within one enum*, and why the prose matters:

- **Fortress Biotech / CUTX-101 (Menkes disease)** — CRL Oct 1 2025 for **manufacturing-site cGMP
  deficiencies**, then **approved via a Class-1 resubmission Jan 13 2026**. Precedent lesson: a
  CMC-only CRL on a drug with clean efficacy is a *timeline event, often a buy-the-dip*, not a thesis
  break.
- **Pierre Fabre / tabelecleucel (tab-cel)** — CRL where **FDA reversed its own position**, no longer
  accepting a previously-agreed single-arm study for accelerated approval and demanding a new study —
  *"a significant and unexpected change in position."* Precedent lesson: an efficacy/design CRL,
  especially an FDA reversal, is a *deep, slow, re-underwrite-the-whole-asset* event.
  ([Pierre Fabre CRL release](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/pierre-fabre-pharmaceuticals-statement-regarding-receipt-of-complete-response-letter-for-tabelecleucel-biologics-license-application-from-the-us-food-and-drug-administration-302658114.html);
  [medpath: how CRLs reshape valuations](https://trial.medpath.com/news/fda-complete-response-letters-how-crls-reshape-biotech-valuations-and-drug-development-timelines)).

**What makes the "why" reusable (the extraction rules):**
1. **Name the class, not just the instance** — "CMC/third-party-fill-finish CRL," "efficacy/endpoint
   CRL," "FDA-reversal-of-prior-agreement CRL." A class is retrievable; an anecdote isn't. (Mauboussin's
   reference-class rule, §1c.)
2. **State the base rate for that class** — "CMC-only CRLs resolve to approval within ~6–12 months in
   the majority of cases" — so the next analyst inherits a prior instead of re-guessing.
3. **Situate it in the specific history** — this management's prior FDA interactions, this endpoint's
   precedent at this division, the competitive/label context — the *inside view* that adjusts the base
   rate up or down.
4. **Make it price-relative** — tie the "why" to the *expectation gap* (how much surprise vs what was
   priced/implied), per Mauboussin §1c, because the tradeable lesson is about surprise, not the event.
5. **End in a rule** — one transferable sentence a future case can apply (the Bridgewater "machine"
   fix, §1d).

## 3. The post-mortem / decision-journal structure (the "after" narrative)

### 3a. The decision-journal fields (Farnam Street / Kahneman lineage)

Farnam Street's decision-journal template — refined from Kahneman on calibration — records, *before
acting*: **the situation/context; the problem statement/frame; the variables that govern it; the
complications/complexity; alternatives seriously considered and why rejected; the range of outcomes;
what you expect to happen with reasoning and actual probabilities; and your physical/mental state at
decision time.** *"The key is looking in this mirror before you act, not after,"* then reviewing weeks
later to compare prediction vs reality and catch bias
([fs.blog decision journal](https://fs.blog/decision-journal/);
[Farnam Street template PDF](https://www.scribd.com/document/701938247/Decision-Template)).
Our warm playbook is built with hindsight, so we don't get the genuine "before" — but we *reconstruct*
the knowable-as-of "before" (RESEARCH-C's `knowable_asof`) and pair it with the real "after."

### 3b. Premortem (Klein/Kahneman) — the disconfirmer field

The premortem inverts the question from *"what might go wrong?"* to *"it's a year later and this failed
— why?"* — the grammatical shift to past tense produces ~30% more candid failure modes and is the
technique Kahneman named his most valued
([McKinsey: premortems](https://www.mckinsey.com/capabilities/strategy-and-corporate-finance/our-insights/bias-busters-premortems-being-smart-at-the-start);
[fs.blog: Kahneman](https://fs.blog/kahneman-better-decisions/);
[get-alfred premortem](https://get-alfred.ai/blog/pre-mortem-technique)). This is where our
**`what_would_change_our_mind` / disconfirmers** field comes from — and, critically, the *post-mortem*
checks which premortem failure mode actually fired.

### 3c. Process-not-outcome (the anti-resulting rule) — the single most important post-mortem field

Every serious source insists a post-mortem grade the **decision, not the result**: *"good decisions can
lead to bad outcomes… evaluate decisions, not outcomes,"* because randomness makes outcomes "silent on
decision quality," and self-attribution bias makes us bank wins as skill and dump losses on luck
([CFA Institute: luck vs skill](https://blogs.cfainstitute.org/investor/2025/02/21/luck-vs-skill-great-investment-leaders-know-the-difference/);
[Evidence Investor: self-attribution bias](https://www.evidenceinvestor.com/post/luck-pretending-to-be-skill-self-attribution-bias-in-active-management)).
For a binary biotech event this is acute: you can be *right on process* (correctly judged the trial
underpowered, sized it small, hedged with a peer) and still lose on a coin-flip, or *right for the
wrong reason.* The post-mortem must separate: **was the outcome surprising vs the knowable-as-of prior**
(a data fact) from **was our reasoning sound** (a process judgment). Only the second teaches the next
trade.

## 4. RECOMMENDATION — the narrative fields a catalyst-outcome node should carry

Design principles distilled from §§1–3:
- **Two blocks.** A **`narrative` block on the `catalyst` node** (the reconstructed knowable-as-of
  thesis/why) and a **`postmortem` block on the `outcome` node** (what happened, why, and the rule).
  This respects the C schema (drivers live beside them in the same `attrs_json`) and the bitemporal
  split (thesis = knowable-as-of; post-mortem = after resolution).
- **Short.** VIC/decision-brief register: each field is 1–3 sentences. The whole thing should read in
  under a minute. Long = unusable = un-retrieved.
- **Class + base rate + rule** in every field where it applies — that's what makes it precedent (§2).
- **The `thesis` field is the embedded string** (RESEARCH-C §0 `kb_vec`), so it must be self-contained.
- **Cite-or-drop** (project invariant): each narrative field's claims trace to the `CITES` evidence on
  the node; the prose paraphrases, the edges hold the source.

### Block A — on the `catalyst` node (`attrs.narrative`), knowable-as-of

| Field | Holds | Target length | Biotech-CRL example |
|---|---|---|---|
| `thesis` *(embedded)* | The one-line variant view as an expectation gap: our PoS vs the priced-in/implied move. Self-contained (names company, asset, catalyst, direction). | 1–2 sentences | "PDUFA for Xdrug (Menkes): options imply a ±35% move pricing ~coin-flip approval, but our read of the clean pivotal + non-controversial mechanism puts PoS well above that — the gap is on approval odds, not efficacy." |
| `causal_setup` | The situation & the causal factors in play *as of the decision*: endpoint/design quality, mechanism precedent, FDA/division history, safety, runway, competitive/label context — in prose. | 2–4 sentences | "Single Phase 3 met its primary with a large effect; the class has prior FDA approvals; the only visible risk is a third-party fill-finish site with a prior Form 483." |
| `reference_class` | The named class this case belongs to + its base rate (the outside view). | 1 sentence + a number | "Class: first-cycle PDUFA, rare-disease, clean-efficacy, single-CMC-risk. Base rate of first-cycle approval for this class ≈ historically high; CMC is the only swing factor." |
| `variant_why` | *Why* our view differs from the market — what the market is over/under-weighting, per §1c. | 1–2 sentences | "Market is over-weighting a generic 'small-cap PDUFA = binary' prior and under-weighting that CMC-only risk is fixable and efficacy is not in question." |
| `what_would_change_our_mind` | Premortem disconfirmers: the specific observations that would break the thesis (§3b). | 2–3 bullets | "A pre-PDUFA Form 483 escalation; an AdCom being scheduled late; label/CI leak suggesting an efficacy concern we missed." |

### Block B — on the `outcome` node (`attrs.postmortem`), after resolution

| Field | Holds | Target length | Biotech-CRL example |
|---|---|---|---|
| `what_happened` | The factual outcome in one line: label + the *specific* sub-type, not just the enum. | 1 sentence | "CRL issued citing cGMP deficiencies at the contract fill-finish site only; no efficacy or safety deficiency cited." |
| `why_narrative` | The causal story situated in precedent — class + history + the specific driver that fired (§2). The heart of the record. | 3–5 sentences | "This is a CMC-only CRL, the fixable class: FDA took no issue with the pivotal or safety, only the third-party site's compliance — echoing Fortress/CUTX-101, where a CMC CRL (Oct-2025) resolved to approval via a Class-1 resubmission (Jan-2026). The prior Form 483 was the tell we flagged; the efficacy thesis is intact. Contrast the tab-cel case, where FDA reversed on trial design — a different, existential class." |
| `surprise_vs_prior` | Data fact: how the outcome compared to the knowable-as-of prior and the priced-in move (anti-resulting input, §3c). | 1–2 sentences | "Outcome was adverse vs our high approval prior but the −40% move over-reacted: it priced a thesis-break when the deficiency is a ~6-month CMC fix." |
| `process_grade` | Process-not-outcome judgment: was the *reasoning* sound independent of the result; right/wrong, and right-for-the-right-reason? (§3c) | 1–2 sentences | "Process partially sound: we correctly identified CMC as the only risk and named the 483, but under-weighted its probability and should have sized smaller / hedged the CMC leg." |
| `which_disconfirmer_fired` | Which premortem disconfirmer actually occurred (links `what_would_change_our_mind` → reality). | 1 sentence | "The 'Form 483 escalation' disconfirmer fired; the AdCom and efficacy-leak disconfirmers did not." |
| `lesson_rule` | The one transferable rule for the next case — the Bridgewater 'machine' fix; this is what promotes into a `playbook` node (§1d). | 1 sentence, imperative | "When a clean-efficacy PDUFA has an open third-party-CMC 483, treat CRL risk as material-but-fixable: size smaller, and treat a CMC-only CRL dip as a resubmission-timeline buy, not a thesis break." |
| `second_order` | Downstream/competitive effects the outcome sets in motion (optional, when present). | 1–2 sentences | "Resubmission pushes launch ~6 months, ceding first-mover window to a competitor's overlapping PDUFA; watch peer re-rating." |

### Notes on fit & frugality
- All eleven fields are strings/lists inside existing `attrs_json` — **zero schema change** (RESEARCH-C
  §1). `thesis` is the string fed to `local_embed` for `kb_vec`; the rest ride along for prompt context.
- `reference_class` + `why_narrative` are what make the record *retrievable-as-precedent*: they carry
  the class label and base rate that `analog_query` results should surface into the prompt block.
- `lesson_rule` is the bridge to generalization: harvest distinct `lesson_rule`s per
  `(scenario_type, class)` into `insert_playbook` scenarios (`kb.py:252`).
- Population is LLM-extracted from the same cached 8-K/PR/CT.gov material B uses for structured
  attribution — **same pass, one extra prompt section** — so no new external calls (frugality).
- Optional trims if token budget bites: `second_order` and `which_disconfirmer_fired` are the two
  droppable-first fields; `thesis`, `why_narrative`, `reference_class`, `process_grade`, `lesson_rule`
  are the irreducible core.
