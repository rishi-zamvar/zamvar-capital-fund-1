The exit engine we built (Tier-3 Task A) turned out to be cutting our winners — selling out of the exact catalyst events the strategy exists to hold. On an identical set of trades it destroyed roughly 810 percentage-points of return versus simply holding the week. One disciplined, one-line fix removed all of that damage. Here is the full night.

02The negative result

The desk takes a ~1-week position a day or two before a binary biotech catalyst (an FDA decision, a trial readout). The whole thesis is the event. The exit engine was supposed to protect those positions with pre-committed stop / take-profit / band levels. Instead, priced against real daily bars, it did this:

Weekly hold (baseline)+12.68%47.9%+33.6%−6.6%

The tell was in the averages: average win collapsed from +34% to +9% while average loss nearly tripled. The engine was cutting winners short and holding losers — the textbook signature of stops firing on noise. A strange latency result confirmed it: adding execution delay improved returns, which only happens when the exits are firing too early and the position later recovers.

03Why it happened

Root cause — the engine ignored the one rule that matters for this strategy: hold through the event.

Two compounding faults, both confirmed with an instrumented breakdown of every exit:

  • It exited before the thesis paid off. 45 of 48 catalyst trades were stopped or taken-profit on ordinary intraweek wiggles — before or across the very event they were placed for. Of 7 genuine catalyst winners (averaging +107%), the engine cut them to an average of +3.8%.
  • The exit levels were sized for a quiet week, not a binary event. The take-profit sat about 1% above entry — so on a stock that routinely swings several percent into a catalyst, it sold on the first tick and often filled into a reversal. That "take-profit" bucket alone lost money.

04The review that saved us from a worse fix

The analysis proposed four fixes. Before any code was written, an independent adversarial agent — briefed as a career-skeptic hedge-fund PM — tore into them. It killed the tempting-but-dangerous ones:

Proposed fixVerdictWhy
Hold through the eventKeptStructural rule, not a tuned number. Restores the actual thesis.
Add a trailing stopRejectedA chart-technical you explicitly don't trust — and it destroys value on the post-event fade.
Re-anchor the take-profitRejectedRelied on a number the backtest doesn't actually have — a phantom input.
"Catastrophic-gap" guardRejectedA made-up threshold that contradicts our own principle: position size is the defense, not a stop.
The discipline that mattered

The evidence came from just 42 trades over the same history the playbook was built on — a small, optimistically-biased sample. Tuning specific numbers to it would be curve-fitting. So we implemented only the structural rule and tuned nothing. Real calibration waits for the forward paper month, on events the system has never seen.

05The fix, and the edge restored

The surviving fix was a single guard in the shared exit function: a position flagged "hold through the catalyst" no longer takes any price-based exit — it holds to its planned exit date. One line, in one place every trade routes through. Non-catalyst positions are completely unchanged; the weekly baseline is untouched.

06The insight worth keeping

Exit at the event, not on Friday

Verifying the residual gap revealed something real: the winning behavior on these catalysts was to exit into the event's initial pop — not to hold to the end of the week and give back the fade. That's the desk's own "spend judgment at entry, execute at the event" principle showing up in the data. It's the most promising next lever — an event-anchored exit-timing decision. But it must be proven on the forward paper month, never fit to these 42 in-sample trades.

07The Task B decision

The original next build was a "judgment layer": an agent that reasons about surprise outcomes the pre-set plan doesn't cover.

Defer  Build it later — not now.

These "off-map" surprises were 0.0% of trades after the fix. Building the reasoning agent today would be polishing a rounding error while the real money was in the on-map timing we just fixed. The genuine next lever is the exit-timing question above — and the honest place to settle it is the paper month, not a biased backtest. The fixed exit engine is ready for that paper month as-is.

08Honest caveats

  • The backtest is optimistic by design. It runs over the same history the playbook was built on, so trust the direction (the engine was harmful; the fix removes the harm), not the exact magnitudes.
  • Small sample. 42 catalyst trades, 7 real winners. Enough to diagnose a structural bug; not enough to tune parameters. That's why we didn't.
  • The real proof is still ahead. One month of forward paper trading, on events the system has never seen, remains the only true test of edge — exactly as planned.
