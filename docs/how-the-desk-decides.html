01The catalyst this week — CAPR

Capricor Therapeutics faces an FDA approval decision (a "PDUFA date") on 22 August 2026 for deramiocel, a cell therapy for the heart damage in Duchenne muscular dystrophy. It's a classic single-drug binary event — the kind the desk is built to trade.

Why this one is treacherous

The company's confirmatory trial (HOPE-3) actually hit its goals in Dec 2025 (54% slowing of muscle decline, 91% slowing of heart decline). But on 29 July 2026 an FDA advisory panel voted 3–9 against the drug's effectiveness — after seeing that very data. The stock, once $22.43, had crashed to ~$4 and then bounced ~79% to $6.65 — but that bounce was short-sellers covering (27% of the float was short), not a vote of confidence.

Workflow 1b / 2 · Trade Builder + Risk
Builder → Risk Committee → PM

If the direction is tradeable, a builder agent sizes the expected move from a "football field" of five independent methods; a three-view risk committee argues sizing and hedging; deterministic tools then overwrite the size with a survival-first cap (you must survive the worst case). A PM agent promotes or kills.

03The overarching decision

All five frameworks exist to answer one question honestly: should we take this one-week trade, and how much? The chain is deliberately adversarial — analysts can be over-optimistic, so a bear argues back; the first desk can be wrong, so an independent diligence firewall can veto; both can miss something, so the MD reconciles; and none of it moves money without a human.

CAPR — the desk's actual decision this week

Negative · conf 0.62 → No trade

The desk read the FDA decision as likely negative — anchored on the 3–9 advisory vote taken with the good data in hand — and, unable to express a bearish view on a long-only book, correctly declined to trade. It also flagged, honestly, that no cited base rate exists for how often the FDA overrides its own panel — so it treated "panel said no → FDA says no" as an inference, not a certainty, and made that a kill-criterion.

What this shows — and its honest limits

The agents produce genuine, cited, self-critical reasoning — not a black-box score. But two honest caveats: (1) the desk's edge is still unproven — the only real test is a forward paper month on events it has never seen; and (2) the desk trades only what it can express — a long-only book skips the many negative-outcome catalysts, taking the ~1-in-3 clean positive setups where the odds and the survival math both line up.
