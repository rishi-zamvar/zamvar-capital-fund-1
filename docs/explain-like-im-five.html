How our money robot works

And the simple list of what's left before it can do its big practice run.

We're building a careful robot helper. It watches special medicine companies. Every so often a company has a big day — like a doctor saying "yes, your medicine is allowed!" On that day the company can suddenly become worth a lot more, or a lot less. Our robot tries to guess which way before the big day, so we can try to earn a little bit each week. And it writes down what happens every time, so it keeps getting smarter.

🧩 What we've already built

Think of it as a little town of helpers. Here's who lives there:

📋 The watch-list

A list of companies we keep our eyes on. Right now it's a small list — soon we'll make it bigger so there's a big day to look at almost every week.

Like: your list of favorite teams to watch.

🔍 The fact-collecting robots

Little helpers that go to free public places and copy down facts — how much each company is worth, when their big days are, how much money they have left. They never make things up. If they can't find something, they say "I don't know" instead of guessing.

Like: helpers who go to the library and carefully copy down real facts.

👯 Two robots check each other

For the important numbers, two different robots go get the same fact. If they agree — great, we trust it. If they disagree, we get a little warning instead of believing a wrong number.

Like: asking two friends the same question to be sure.

📨 Our own big-day finder

The cleverest robot reads the companies' own official letters and finds the exact big-day dates — straight from the source, for free. This was a big win: other people charge money for these dates, and we don't have to pay.

Like: reading the birthday invitation instead of guessing the date.

🧑‍🔬 The pretend-expert team

A group of make-believe experts studies each company: a science expert, a rules expert, a money expert. Two of them argue — one says "it'll go up!", the other says "it'll go down!" — and a boss listens and decides. Every fact they use has to show where it came from, or it doesn't count.

Like: two kids debating, and a grown-up making the final call.

🛡️ The safety grown-up (you!)

Before any real money is ever used, a real person has to say "yes." Always. And we practice with pretend money first.

Like: a seatbelt that's always on.

📓 The notebook (the part that gets smarter)

Every time something happens, we write down three things: what we thought before, what actually happened, and why. Next time, the team reads the notebook and does a little better than last time.

Like: writing down what worked so you win more next time.

🚚 How the fact-collecting works

Here's the whole fact-collecting trip, step by step:

  1. The robots go grab facts from lots of free public places.
  2. Two robots double-check the important numbers with each other.
  3. All the facts go into one tidy toy box, each with a note saying where it came from.
  4. The expert team only reads from that one tidy box — so everyone uses the same clean facts.
  5. A timer robot does this again and again, so the box is always fresh. If one robot's place is closed, another covers for it — so it never stops.

🎯 What's left before the big practice run

The big practice means going back through last year, one week at a time, and letting the team make a pick each week — like practicing a game by replaying last season. Four things to do first:

Step 1

👀 Watch more companies

Right now there aren't enough big days to practice with. We'll add more companies until there's about one good big day every week — roughly 52 in a year.

Step 2

📅 Collect last year's big days

Go back and gather all the big days from the past year — and what actually happened on each one (did the price jump up, or down?).

Step 3

📓 Get the notebook ready

Set up the notebook so every practice pick gets written down — the before, the after, and the why — and so each new pick can read all the earlier lessons.

Step 4

🔁 Do the practice — 52 times

Go week by week through last year. Each week: the team makes a pick, we see what really happened, write it in the notebook, and the team gets a little smarter. By the end we have a well-worn notebook and finely-tuned tools.

💡 One honest thing to remember

🌱 The practice is for learning, not for bragging

The big practice run mostly fills up the notebook and tunes the tools. It's okay if the team peeks a little at old answers while it practices — we're using it to learn, not to show off.

The real test comes after: we play the game for real, but with pretend money, for a whole month — going forward, where the team can't peek because it hasn't happened yet. That's when we find out if our robot is actually any good. Only if it passes that do we ever talk about real money — with you saying "yes" first.
