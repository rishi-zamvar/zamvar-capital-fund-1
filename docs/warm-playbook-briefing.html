A clean win averages +46%. A miss averages −41%. But more of them miss — so the edge is never “which stock,” it’s “which direction, and is it already in the price.”

01 The edges we distilled

Eight patterns recur across the corpus often enough to trade around. Each names what happens, why, how common it is, real examples, and what the desk should actually do.

Sell-the-News on De-Risked Data

Fade the pop

When the market already expects good data, a clean win falls flat — or drops.

The information is in the price before the print. “Met primary” only re-rates a stock when it resolves real doubt; once de-risked, the readout removes a catalyst (positioning unwinds) instead of adding value.

≈32% of events VERA −71% · clean win, huge run-up IDYA −4% SUPN −2% · known since 2020

Play Buy the uncertainty pre-catalyst, sell into strength. Fade the pop on confirmatory readouts and commoditized approvals — the tradeable catalyst is the first pivotal or the partner decision, not the confirmation.

The Single-Asset Falling Knife

Avoid / define risk

A microcap that is one drug on an unproven target misses, collapses 70–90%, and keeps falling.

No pipeline to cushion the miss; distress forces a dilutive raise straight into the crater, so the equity re-rates to option value with no floor.

14 of the 26 worst misses KALA −93% AVTX −90% HURA −89% ACRS −83%

Play Never a core long into a single-asset unvalidated binary. If you must, express it with defined-risk options only — and never buy the post-miss “bounce.”

The Benchmark-Gap Miss

Fade “primary met”

A drug can hit its endpoint with p<0.05 and still crater — because the effect size landed below the class bar the market was pricing.

The market prices the competitive magnitude (class hazard ratio / % reduction), not the binary p-value. Statistical significance is necessary, not sufficient; magnitude-vs-class is the real verdict.

≈19% of events GOSS −77% · hit, below class ALKS −10% APGE −22%

Play Know the indication’s class effect-size bar before the readout. Fade a “primary met” release whenever the disclosed magnitude undershoots it — treat it as a miss regardless of the p-value.

The Addressable Rerun / Second Pivotal

Go long

A well-powered confirmatory or a rerun of a trial whose prior failure was specific and fixable is the corpus’s most reliable big winner.

Prior de-risking shifts the true odds sharply positive while the market still prices residual failure risk — so the gap closes violently on a clean hit.

the right tail of 97 hits (avg +46%) LYRA +363% · 2nd pivotal SPRO +267% · rerun post-CRL CAPR +323%

Play The cleanest positive-EV long in the book: the well-powered rerun where the prior failure cause was addressed and the market still discounts it.

Distressed Convexity (the Near-Zero Lottery)

Defined-risk basket

Beaten-down near-zero microcaps that actually hit go parabolic — the mirror image of the falling knife.

When the equity already prices failure, a hit is pure optionality re-rate plus a short squeeze. The downside was already realized, so the payoff is wildly asymmetric.

small cohort, extreme right tail PROK +753% SRRK +274% NKTR +211%

Play A small, equal-weight, defined-risk call basket on distressed names with a genuinely addressable thesis — sized for total loss each. The tail winners pay for the basket.

The PDUFA Anchor Artifact

Trust the outcome, not the label

Several “rejection” records in our data were actually FDA approvals that sold off — the label was stamped from the price move, not the real decision.

A mechanical rule labels any negative-move approval decision a “CRL.” But approvals routinely sell off when priced-in or commoditized — so a real approval can print red and get mislabeled a rejection.

all 16 “crl” labels; ≥4 were real approvals CRSP −17% · CASGEVY approved MCRB −21% LPCN −28%

Play Approval is not an automatic long — in crowded markets, sell the approval. And this is a data-quality fix on our list: replace move-anchored labels with the scraped FDA decision (see caveats).

The Broken Dose-Response

Fade non-monotonic curves

When the market expects a clean “more dose, more effect” curve and gets a jagged or inverted one, the stock sells off even if the primary was met.

A monotonic curve is the market’s causality test. Non-monotonicity reads as noise or placebo artifact and undermines belief in the mechanism — overriding a nominal hit.

≈4% — thin but sharp & pre-identifiable NBIX −17% PRAX −65% OCGN −7%

Play Pre-register the expected dose-response as a watch item. Fade on a broken curve even if the headline says “primary met” — the market will.

Pre-Priced Run-Up

Don’t chase strength

Readouts whose topline was effectively pre-announced — or names that ran up hard into the event — disappoint on the actual print regardless of quality.

The datum is already in the tape, so the event resolves nothing new; the run-up itself becomes the risk as positioning unwinds.

≈8%, overlaps sell-the-news SUPN −2% VERA −71%

Play Score the pre-catalyst run-up. Don’t initiate longs into names already up big, and prefer the fade if the topline was previously disclosed.

02 Which setups have the odds

The same reference classes recur. Grouped by how the odds — and the trade — actually look going in.

Green · favorable
  • Confirmatory Phase 3 in a validated mechanism (prior positive Ph2, known target) — high hit rate. CELC, CAPR, SRRK.
  • Well-powered rerun of a trial whose prior CRL/failure was specific & addressed — the re-rate is large. LYRA, SPRO.
  • Clean resubmissions / already-approved-ex-US — high approval odds (but watch the trade: may still sell off if priced-in).
Amber · it depends
  • Competitive indications where effect-size-vs-class is the crux (IgAN, obesity, dry eye) — resolves on magnitude, easily “mixed.”
  • Subgroup- or composite-driven pivotals with placebo exposure — foreseeable splits; down-weight subgroup-only wins.
  • De-risked confirmatory & priced-in approvals — good science, poor trade; expect flat-to-negative even on a hit.
  • Biomarker-primary where the real bar is a downstream clinical endpoint — a surrogate win can still miss the decisive variable.
Red · ugly odds
  • Single-asset microcap, first-in-class, unvalidated target, cash-stressed — the worst blowups. KALA, ACRS, AVTX, QTTB.
  • Low-base-rate neuro/onc (GBM, ALS, Alzheimer’s) on a single asset — the class base rate is the risk. HURA, LONA, AMLX.
  • Efficacy carried by subgroups while the overall primary is modest — reads as a miss. GOSS.
  • Commoditized-market approvals — approval is expected and sells off.

04 How the desk actually uses this

The playbook isn’t a report we read — it’s memory the trading agents reason from, live, every week.

RETRIEVE

Find the analogs

A new catalyst pulls its nearest past events from this memory — same type, same kind of setup — each with its real outcome.

ASSEMBLE

Build the field

Those analogs become one column of the “football field” of expected move — alongside hard valuations (rNPV, price targets) once wired.

JUDGE

Apply the edge

The lessons here — priced-in? benchmark gap? falling knife? — adjust the direction and the size before anything is traded.

LEARN

Write it back

Every new week’s outcome becomes another node — the memory compounds, and the base rates sharpen.

05 Ideas this opens

The patterns above aren’t just filters — each is a screen or signal we could build next.

A “priced-in” score

Combine pre-event run-up with de-risking flags (prior Ph2 win, ex-US approval, disclosed topline) into one number that flags names likely to sell the news — the fade screen.

A benchmark-gap detector

Keep a per-indication class effect-size table; at readout, auto-compare the disclosed magnitude to the bar and flag “met-primary-but-below-class” as a fade even when the PR is glowing.

A falling-knife filter

Tag single-asset + microcap + short-runway + unvalidated-target names as “avoid / defined-risk-only,” and suppress every post-miss bounce-buy signal on them.

A convexity lottery basket

The inverse screen: distressed near-zero names with a specific addressable thesis get a small equal-weight call basket, sized for total loss each — the tails fund it.

PDUFA de-anchoring

Replace move-stamped labels with the scraped real FDA decision, then build an “approval-that-sells-off” sub-model to fade priced-in or commoditized approvals.

A rerun green-screen

Auto-flag well-powered reruns where a prior failure cause was specific and addressed and the market still discounts it — paired with a dose-response watch that fades broken curves.

06 What to keep in mind (honesty)

The playbook is powerful but not infallible. The things you should not over-trust:

  • The “rejection” label is not ground truth. All 16 “crl” labels are stamped from a negative price move, not the FDA’s decision — several were real approvals that sold off. We know the fix (scrape the actual decision) and it’s queued.
  • The label is outcome-vs-expectation, not the direction of the move. 21 of 97 “hits” actually fell; some “misses” rose. Never read profit-and-loss from the label alone.
  • A few records aren’t really trial readouts — some are acquisitions, financings, or content-free conference decks that happened to sit near the date. Their move isn’t about the drug.
  • Small samples are directional only. Approvals (29), the dose-response cohort (~10) — treat their base rates as hints, not laws.
  • This is a hindsight library by design. The base rates are backward-selected (biased-OK for finding patterns). Only forward paper-trading proves an edge actually persists.
