Trial-results days are by far the most common event — roughly 3-to-1 over FDA decision days. That's exactly why counting only PDUFAs gave us too small a universe: we were ignoring the biggest, most frequent source of biotech stock moves.

But "collected" ≠ "usable" — the precision gap

To measure how a stock moved, an event needs (a) to have already happened and (b) a specific known day. Many don't yet.

The key nuance PDUFA dates are almost all pin-down-able (every one carries a filing day), so they're cleanly measurable. Trial readouts are trickier: ~half come from the trials registry with only a rough month ("Q2 2026") or are still upcoming — those can't be measured. That's why we added a second source for readouts — the result announcement filings, which carry a real day — to turn more of that 615 into measurable events. That conversion is what the running scan is doing right now.

The result-announcement filings now being pulled carry the real reaction day; once those dominate, the readout figure should climb toward the double-digit range these events are known for. What the full enrichment will add

Per event type: typical move, the spread (how reliable), up-vs-down skew, and how the move differs by trial phase and by whether the news was a hit or a miss. That per-type movement profile is what lets the desk size a trade to the event it's actually facing — and it feeds directly into the playbook.
