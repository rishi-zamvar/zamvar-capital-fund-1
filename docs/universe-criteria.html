Universe & Hedge-Peer Criteria

Two screens, not one. (A) the ~20 core names you trade around catalysts, and (B) the hedge peers that must rise when a target falls. Thresholds below are calibrated starting points — tune them against the backtest. Every criterion maps to a data source you can research today.

The one idea that governs everything: a real hedge is a peer whose stock goes up when your name's catalyst goes badly — a competitive-displacement relationship, not mere correlation. Most of the hard work is in Screen B. A merely "correlated" peer is not a hedge; a peer that rises on your success (positive class read-through) is the opposite of a hedge.

A Core tradeable universe — the ~20 names

These are the catalyst subjects. The screen selects names where a single, dateable, binary event moves the stock materially and is analyzable from real data.

1 · Catalyst density

≥ 2 dateable binary catalysts / yr
The engine only fires on events. A name with one vague readout a year can't earn its slot. Count hard, dated events: PDUFA, AdCom, Ph2/Ph3 topline readouts, key conference data (ASCO/ESMO/AHA), pivotal interim analyses.
Research: ClinicalTrials.gov (phase, primary-completion dates) · FDA/PDUFA trackers · company IR "upcoming milestones" decks · conference abstract calendars

2 · Catalyst dateability & source-verifiability

date confirmable from ≥ 2 sources
A wrong date is a total loss. The name qualifies only if its catalysts have dates you can verify from two independent sources and re-check 48h out. Windows (e.g. "1H 2027") are allowed but must be typed as windows, never treated as firm.
Research: cross-check ClinicalTrials.gov vs. company guidance vs. a PDUFA tracker; discard names whose catalysts are perpetually "expected soon" with no anchor

3 · Market-cap band

~ $200M – $10B
Small/mid-cap clinical-stage is the sweet spot: one asset's outcome actually moves the whole company. Large pharma dilutes a single readout into noise; sub-$200M micro-caps bring manipulation, halts, and reverse-split risk.
Research: FMP market cap; bias toward single-asset / lead-asset-dominant companies where the catalyst is existential

4 · Liquidity

ADV ≥ ~$5–10M · spread tight
You need to build and exit a one-week position without the spread eating the edge. Avg daily dollar volume, not share volume. Screen out chronic-halt names and anything with a persistently wide bid/ask.
Research: FMP quote/volume history (compute 30-day ADV) · check historical halt frequency

5 · Single-catalyst attributability

one identifiable driver
The move must be attributable to ONE event, so the desk's call and the P&L attribution are clean. Names whose price is a muddle of pipeline + financing + partnership noise are hard to model and hard to hedge.
Research: map the near-term event calendar; flag names with overlapping catalysts in the same window (ambiguous attribution)

6 · Historical binary magnitude

peers move ±30–70% on the event class
Enough move to clear costs and hit the weekly target, but you must respect that the same magnitude is the downside. This sizes the risk-of-ruin math; it does not justify upsizing.
Research: event study — pull the name's (and peers') 1–3 day returns around past readouts of the same type

7 · Therapeutic-area spread

diversify across ≥ 4 areas
A book stacked in one biology (e.g. all oncology) is one scientific surprise away from a correlated drawdown. Spread across oncology, CNS, rare disease, immunology/inflammation, cardio-metabolic.
Research: tag each name by lead indication + mechanism; enforce area caps at the portfolio layer

8 · Data availability for the desk's tools

on CT.gov + FMP + has FDA history
If the analysts and quant tools can't get trial design, financials, and regulatory history, the name can't be analyzed rigorously — exclude it. Coverage IS a selection criterion.
Research: confirm the ticker resolves on FMP (fundamentals/float/SI) and its trials are on ClinicalTrials.gov with a clean lead-sponsor legal name

9 · Cash-runway sanity

runway clears the catalyst (or runway IS the catalyst)
You want the catalyst — not a surprise dilutive raise or going-concern collapse — to drive the move. Either the company is funded past the event, or the financing risk is itself the thesis and modeled explicitly.
Research: FMP cash + quarterly burn → runway in quarters; read the latest 10-Q "going concern" language

10 · Optionability (forward-looking)

listed options w/ real OI — nice-to-have
Not required for the equities-only v1, but names with liquid options give you the implied-move signal for diligence and a path to true put-hedges later. Note it; don't gate on it yet.
Research: Polygon options (later) — open interest + implied move around events

11 · Tradeable venue

US-listed common (RH-supported)
Must be executable through the Robinhood equities MCP. US-listed common/ADR only; no OTC/pink, no instruments RH can't trade.
Research: confirm exchange listing (NASDAQ/NYSE); exclude OTC tickers

B Hedge-peer criteria — the inverse-payoff screen

For each core name, you need candidate peers that rise when the target falls on its catalyst. This is the crux of the whole strategy — a long-only hedge only works through competitive displacement.

1 · Direct competitive overlap

same indication + patient population
The peer must compete for the same market. If the target's drug fails, the surviving competitor captures that opportunity → its stock rises. Same indication, ideally same line of therapy and overlapping mechanism-of-action battleground.
Research: build the competitive landscape per indication (CT.gov by condition; analyst/industry landscape maps; company "competition" risk-factor sections)

2 · Conditional INVERSE payoff on THIS catalyst

peer ↑ when target ↓ (event-conditional)
Not unconditional correlation — the payoff conditional on the specific outcome. Estimate a matrix: target fails → peer +X%; target wins → peer −Y%. If the peer doesn't reliably move opposite on this event type, it provides zero protection.
Research: event studies of past head-to-head readouts in the indication (how did the competitor trade on the rival's success/failure?)

3 · Catalyst-clean over the hold window

NO own catalyst during the ~1-week hold
The single most important biotech-specific screen. If the peer has its own PDUFA / readout / earnings / AdCom / M&A rumor in the window, its move is driven by its own noise, not the target's event — and your "hedge" becomes a second uncontrolled bet.
Research: run the peer through the same catalyst calendar; reject if any event falls in the hold window

4 · Read-through is opposite, not shared

DISQUALIFY positive class read-through
The subtle killer. For novel-mechanism or first-in-class assets, a target's success often lifts the whole class (validation read-through) — so the "peer" rises with the target, not against it. Those are not hedges. Only zero-sum competitive relationships (my win = your loss) qualify.
Research: ask "if the target succeeds, does this peer go up or down?" — if up, it's disqualified as a hedge (may still be a correlated long, but not protection)

5 · Cointegration / stable long-run relationship

Engle-Granger / Johansen passes
For the sector-beta portion of the hedge, the pair should share a real, mean-reverting long-run price relationship — not a spurious correlation that decouples exactly when you need it.
Research: the desk's cointegration tool runs this; for your prep, eyeball a multi-year price-ratio chart for stability

6 · Executable liquidity & size parity

peer ADV supports the hedge leg
A hedge you can't fill isn't a hedge. The peer must be liquid enough to take the hedge-side size without slippage swamping the protection.
Research: FMP ADV on the peer; ensure it clears the same liquidity bar as Screen A

7 · No confounding shared exposure

not co-dependent on the same third party
If target and peer both hinge on the same platform, partner, supplier, or regulatory ruling, shared bad news sinks both — the hedge fails on common-mode risk. The relationship must be competitive, not co-dependent.
Research: check partnerships, licensors, shared platforms/CDMOs, and whether one AdCom/guidance affects both

8 · Sufficient historical analog data

enough past co-events to estimate payoff
The conditional-payoff estimate needs evidence. If there's no history of how the peer moved on comparable rival events, the inverse relationship is a guess — and the desk must fall back, not pretend.
Research: collect past analog events into the KB; thin evidence → flag for size-tiny / exit-before-event
Fallback rule (built into the desk): when no peer clears criteria 1–8, the trade is not hedged with a fake peer — it's flagged to size tiny or exit before the event resolves. "No valid hedge" is a valid, expected outcome.

✕ Hard disqualifiers (exclude on sight)

OTC / pink-sheet / sub-liquidity

Not RH-tradeable at size; manipulation and halt risk. Applies to both screens.

Unverifiable or single-source catalyst date

A mis-sourced date is a total loss — the DATA layer excludes it from trading automatically.

Mid-acquisition / active M&A

Price is deal-driven, not catalyst-driven; the model doesn't apply.

Going-concern / imminent delisting / reverse-split-prone

Financing collapse swamps the catalyst signal (unless runway risk is the explicit thesis).

Hedge peer with positive read-through to the target

Rises on the target's success → not a hedge. Disqualified from Screen B (see B-4).

↳ Per-name research workflow

A repeatable pass to run on each candidate — the output populates data/seed_universe.csv, data/ticker_sponsor_map.csv, and the hedge-relationship store.

  • Pipeline & indication — ClinicalTrials.gov (lead asset, phase, indication, dates, exact lead-sponsor legal name)
  • Regulatory history — FDA / PDUFA-AdCom trackers (prior CRLs, AdCom outcomes) — curate manually
  • Competitive map — who else targets this indication → the hedge-peer candidate set
  • Financials — FMP (market cap, ADV, float, short interest, cash + burn → runway)
  • Event study — historical peer reactions to comparable readouts (the conditional-payoff evidence)
  • Options — Polygon later (OI, implied move) for diligence + future put-hedges
These criteria feed the system directly: Screen A → the universe builder's inclusion thresholds; Screen B → the hedge-relationship sub-store (each peer stored with mechanism + payoff-direction + a citation).
