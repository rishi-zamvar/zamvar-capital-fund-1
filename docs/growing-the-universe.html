Growing the Universe

Why our list of tradeable biotechs came out small, and the two plain moves that grow it into a real set of movers — jargon explained first, then used.

The one idea. We pick which biotechs to trade by looking at how hard their stock has moved on big past moments. We only counted one kind of big moment (FDA approval days), and those are rare — so most companies didn't have enough history to judge, and the list came out to 17 with a weak tail. Count the other kinds of big moments too, and the list grows into real movers.

First: what's a "big moment" for a biotech?

A biotech stock mostly sits still, then lurches on a handful of make-or-break days a year. Those days are what we trade around.

Plain words Catalyst / event = one of those make-or-break days — a piece of news that can send the stock up or down 20–50% in a day. We'll call it an event. There are four main kinds:
✅

FDA decision day jargon: "PDUFA date" we count this

The scheduled day the FDA says yes or no to approving a drug. The biggest, cleanest binary moment. This is the only kind we've been counting so far. They're rare — a company gets one maybe once every year or two.

🧪

Trial results day jargon: "readout" not counted yet

The day a company reveals whether its drug actually worked in a study. Moves the stock just as violently as an approval day — often more. Companies have these more often than FDA decisions. We already collect these; we just aren't using them to score companies.

🗳️

Expert panel vote jargon: "AdCom" not counted yet

Before some FDA decisions, a panel of outside experts publicly votes on whether the drug should be approved. That vote is a preview that itself swings the stock — a real moment in its own right.

📊

Conference presentation jargon: "conference data" not counted yet

Companies unveil results on stage at big medical conferences (cancer, heart, etc.). Those reveals move stocks too. Hardest of the four to collect reliably for free.

What "measurable" means, and why we need two

Plain words Measurable event = an event that (a) already happened, (b) on a specific known day, so (c) we can look up exactly how far the stock jumped or fell around it. An upcoming event isn't measurable yet — it hasn't happened. A vaguely-dated one ("sometime in Q2") can't be pinned to a day.

To decide "does this company reliably move on its events?", one data point isn't enough — a single big jump could be luck. So we require at least two measurable past events before a company can join. That's the bar that protects us from being fooled by one fluke.

So why did the list come out small?

Because we only counted the rarest kind of event.

FDA decision days happen maybe once every year or two per company. Requiring two of them, in just five years of history, most companies simply couldn't clear — so we couldn't score them, and they fell out. Out of 256 biotechs, only 26 had two measurable FDA decisions. That left 17 in the list, several of them barely moving.

It's not that these companies are quiet. It's that we were looking at one narrow slice of their activity.

How we GROW it

Give the system more of the history that's already out there.

Count trial-results days and panel votes too, not just FDA decisions

The single biggest lever. Trial results and expert-panel votes are just as violent as approval days, and companies have far more of them. Counting them means most companies now have two-plus measurable events — so the pool grows past our target, and we can afford to keep only the real movers. We already collect trial results; panel votes we can add.

Look back further than five years

More years = more past events per company. Helps, but older reactions are less like today's, and it's slower to add than the lever above.

How we HARDEN it

Make sure the bigger list is only trustworthy movers — not padded.

Set a floor: a company must typically move ≥10% on its events, or it's out

This deletes the weak tail immediately — names like the one that moved just 1% don't belong in a fund that trades big swings. With a bigger pool behind it, the floor leaves only companies whose events genuinely move the stock.

Keep the two-events-minimum rule

Already in place. It stops one lucky jump from earning a company a spot — every member is proven across multiple events.

Re-scan on a schedule so the list stays current

As new events happen, companies on the "waiting" list earn enough history to join, and any name that's gone quiet can drop out. The universe stays a living, earned list — never a frozen guess.

Plain words The waiting list (we've been calling it the "watch list") = 21 real biotechs that have an upcoming or a single past event, but not yet two measurable ones. They're parked, not rejected — they join automatically once history gives us enough to judge them.

Bottom line, and the one decision

The machine that picks the universe works and is honest — it just fed on too narrow a diet (FDA days only). Feed it the other big-moment types (trial results + panel votes) and add a 10%-move floor, and the same top movers stay, the weak tail drops, and the list grows toward a real ~80-name set of proven movers — enough to give the desk something worth trading most weeks.
